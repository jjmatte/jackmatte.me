The first time I used an LLM to help me in an actual work task I was trying to decipher some vba code written over a decade ago in our database that was housed within Microsoft Access. My boss had mentioned that a developer position was available but that one of his employees would have to prove their capabilities before that role would be theirs. With a growing number of requests from within the company for changes or bug fixes to our forms and reports within the application we needed someone who could functionally do the work. I took this as an opportunity to grow and jumped in.

I had very little problem learning almost any of the systems at work as my experience with low voltage systems (fire alarms, instrumentation etc.) as an Electrician coupled with my many years experience playing around with computers in my free time really allowed me to have a level of expertise my resume would not have reflected. I figured that this would be no exception and within a few hours I was nursing a headache having absolutely no idea where to even begin in this house of cards that had been slowly built over years by multiple people. At some point frustration and a desire for production overcame my hesitancy of relying on an agent and I ran my problem through an agent. Within 15 minutes I had a clear direction and another couple of hours I had solved my first Development problem.

I then spent about 3 months handling various issues within our Access program to varying degrees of success using LLM's to supplement my lack of syntax knowledge as well as the Access application itself. I didn't realize it at the time but I was wading in to a way of coding that would dramatically change the way I work in many ways. At around this time we hired a Senior Developer for the company to migrate our entire application out of Microsoft Access and in to an internally developed Web Application so that we would no longer be reliant on Microsoft Access for continued management of our Database. With this began the first time in my life I was officially employed to develop software.
